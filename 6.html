Overview

Key laws, not the whole lecture

Gram-negative architecture

  • Inner (cytoplasmic) membrane + thin peptidoglycan + outer membrane.
  • Periplasm: gel-filled space between the two membranes.
  • Outer membrane carries LPS: Lipid A – Core polysaccharide – O antigen.
  • No teichoic / lipoteichoic acids.
  • Braun's lipoprotein covalently links outer membrane ↔ peptidoglycan.
  • Almost none form spores.

Why it matters clinically

  • Outer membrane blocks penicillin, detergents, and lysozyme.
  • Lysed bacteria release Lipid A → endotoxin effect.
  • Result: hypotension, respiratory failure, low O₂ delivery, lactic acidosis → septic shock.

Enterobacteriaceae family traits

  • Gram-negative, facultative anaerobic, rod-shaped.
  • Ferment sugars → lactic acid + other products.
  • Mostly motile (flagella); a few genera are non-motile.
  • Fimbriae mediate adhesion to host tissue.
  • Found in gut, mouth, nose, skin, soil, water.

Three genera, one exam

  • Klebsiella — encapsulated, nosocomial, pneumonia/UTI/meningitis/sepsis.
  • E. coli — normal flora that turns pathogenic via 6 diarrheal pathotypes, UTIs, neonatal meningitis.
  • Citrobacter — citrate-user, C. freundii carries inducible ampC resistance.
Klebsiella: oxidase-negative K antigens: 77 serotypes O157:H7: Shiga toxin / bioterrorism agent E. coli meningitis: babies <3 months C. freundii: inducible ampC → ampicillin + 1st-gen cephalosporin resistance
